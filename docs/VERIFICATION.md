All 32 required rows in the finite acceptance matrix passed; E25 remains explicitly optional/unimplemented. The software can check commanded geometry and documented controller semantics; it cannot establish actual machine calibration, tool diameter/runout, stock thickness, workholding, zeroing, or the behaviour of an arbitrary LinuxCNC configuration.

## What is independent

[`tests/oracles/motion.py`](../tests/oracles/motion.py) imports no production geometry code. It describes the physical flat, finite-tip V, and bull-nose lower surfaces with scalar equations. For constant-depth straight travel it computes contact with the entire segment using closest distance, rather than sampling endpoints. For simultaneous depth changes it encloses removal with branch-and-bound intervals and returns an uncertified outcome when its work bound is exhausted. A small tolerance used to resolve numerical intervals is independent of the machine's XY/Z increments and the user's requested fit.

Its swept rectangular bound includes cutter width, clips portions that are physically outside stock as air, and rejects removal outside the permitted stock-intersected rectangle. This is a conservative bounds test; it does not calculate arbitrary fixture collisions. Its travel fixture policy requires lateral noncutting links above stock. It does not certify a below-stock link through previously cleared material. It checks independent XY/Z lattices, movement lengths/feed timing, per-face operation/change counts and asymmetric face registration.

The independent local assembly relation is `board_removed_depth + plug_removed_depth - seating_depth`. Its negative values demonstrate interference at a tested corresponding XY location. For the small inputs, `D=1`, `g=0.2`, `b=0.5` imply seating depth `0.8` and plug height `1.3` mm. Centre and outside-flat references respectively test bottom gap `0.2` and backing clearance `0.5` mm. These scalar probes do not prove every point between probes, arbitrary three-dimensional assembly, or self-seating at nominal pose. The production verifier must still certify the full accepted model; the independent native/analytic checks challenge it through a different implementation.

The topology audit unions removal from every actual tool use on each face. It preserves operation boundaries, so it neither drops earlier roughing removal nor invents a cutting segment between different operations. Its hole and disconnected fixtures use 4 mm/2 mm flat cutters; the mixed fixture uses a flat clearing cutter and a finite-tip V finisher. Expected assembly gaps are specified from the fixture's known interior, counter and exterior reference locations, independently of the solver's target calculations.

[`tests/oracles/stock.py`](../tests/oracles/stock.py) independently classifies local prism material with scalar ray crossings and complete boundary-segment distances. The native capture allowance yields three states: material, air, or uncertain. A path's potential cutting depth in a through-hole does not become removed wood; an absent or uncertain column has no numeric assembly-gap claim. [`audit_stock.py`](../tests/oracles/audit_stock.py) consumes outlines actually captured in the native run, independently checks the known polygon vertices and circular chord sagitta, then creates new paired solver fixtures with those outlines. The annulus has measured maximum chord error about 0.001137 mm within its declared 0.005 mm allowance. Known interior references reproduce 0.2 mm bottom and 0.5 mm backing gaps; hole, notch and absent-collar references remain air. This audit covers flat-tool fixtures and finite material/preview probes, not every point of arbitrary stock or all cutter families. The deliberately constructed air-capsule and unroughed-material controls directly challenge the production mask and engagement boundary using independently known physical outcomes.

[`tests/oracles/native_occt.py`](../tests/oracles/native_occt.py) runs in the actual pinned FreeCAD. It builds revolved analytic cutter sections, extrudes complete cross-sections for constant-Z line sweeps, adds exact endpoint solids, and clips them against native stock. Mathematical volume and area formulae supply expected values. It covers flat, V and bull tools, a complete rounded pocket, an oversized-cutter defect and a missing-depth-pass defect. It also checks all material in a fixed 5×5×1 mm interior core and outside a fixed 9×9 mm boundary envelope of the actual flat solver result: no required core volume remains and no exterior volume is removed. Mutating that actual output produces the independently expected 12.5 mm³ unfinished core; an oversized cutter produces more than 52 mm³ of forbidden exterior removal. These solid checks do not certify the boundary band between those regions or the plug. This implementation deliberately has a 150-segment fixture bound and rejects simultaneous XYZ sweeps. It is separate from the production polygon/slice implementation and is not a general-purpose reference CAM engine.

[`tests/oracles/gcode.py`](../tests/oracles/gcode.py) is a narrow Decimal-based LinuxCNC replay. It independently reconstructs linear absolute/incremental motion, units, feeds, spindle state, selected/loaded tools, work-coordinate selections, tool-length registers and path-control mode. Unknown starting coordinates stay unknown. M6 stops the spindle until restarted; executable content after M2/M30 is rejected. Unsupported coordinates, arcs, expressions and controller-specific features fail explicitly. G61/G61.1 are accepted for the fixture's linear path contract; unrestricted G64 does not receive a geometric certificate. Actual offset register contents, machine motion during tool changes, acceleration and following error are outside this parser's claim.

[`tests/oracles/audit_export.py`](../tests/oracles/audit_export.py) compares replayed exported files with native command snapshots obtained after reopen/edit, independently accounting for FreeCAD's mm/s versus G-code's mm/min feeds. It records SHA256 hashes of input/output files and its own source. This establishes native-to-export preservation, including intentionally detectable precision loss. Its separate feed tolerance defaults to 1e-9 mm/min to accommodate binary mm/s-to-mm/min roundoff; endpoint, spindle, tool, and modal tests remain independent. A regression accepts approximately 2e-14 mm/min conversion roundoff while rejecting 0.00002 mm/min serialization drift, wrong endpoints, tools and G64. It does not by itself establish that native source paths cut a correct inlay; complete E01/E26 need this replay attached to solver-generated, accepted, independently checked geometry.

[`audit_portable_gui.py`](../tests/oracles/audit_portable_gui.py) additionally compares restored operations by identity with the original accepted specification and motion, including the original XY/Z lattice. It isolates the deliberate native `F9` edit and reports other measured native six-decimal feed drift separately. A restored `499.999980` mm/min value is not called exact preservation of an original `500` mm/min input; its `0.000020` difference lies within the independently derived `0.000030` mm/min native-rounding bound. Native-to-export replay must still preserve the actual restored value. Original geometry certification is not transferred to manually edited/reordered CAM paths. The final archive has six completed extension-absent edit/save/reopen audits: multi-flat (retained local receipt), flat (retained local receipt), V (retained local receipt), bull (retained local receipt), flat/V (retained local receipt), and mixed-unit/resolution (retained local receipt). Each passes four actual export comparisons and both original coordinate/lattice comparisons. The mixed-unit fixture retains its 1/8-inch cutter and exact XY=0.05/Z=0.025 mm lattice; its second reopen explicitly reports 508→508.000020 and 1016→1015.999980 mm/min feed drift. Three separate fresh native GUI processes produce independently rejected metric-zero-decimal (retained local receipt), G64 (retained local receipt), and actual G20 inch (retained local receipt) exports. Base portable receipts declare no bundled negative controls; the separate receipts carry those claims. No final run contains the earlier ancillary `setModel` message. Historical native dialog/lifetime failures remain retained and are not counted as passes.

The six [`examples`](../examples/fixture_manifest.json) are local generated vector fixtures with known canvas sizes, topology and analytic areas where available: an asymmetric leaf, an outlined letter with counters, a scattered motif, a fine-detail ladder, a bounds-crossing ornament, and a 10,000-vertex dense field. Production SVG output is compared using independently specified bounds/component counts/holes and a test-owned shoelace-area calculation. These are deterministic examples, not a representative distribution of every SVG editor's output.

## Negative controls and open findings

Pure tests prove that the oracles reject missing finishing depth, oversized cutters, wrong physical mirroring, unsafe lateral links, a changed V depth, incorrect lattices, unknown modal features, post-termination cutting blocks, and precision loss. The actual asymmetric solver-output audit repeats five mutations on real generated motion: missing final board depth produces `-0.3` mm interference; a wrong plug reflection produces `-0.8` mm interference; an oversized cutter removes a known outside point; unsafe travel enters stock; a 60-fold feed error disagrees with independent timing. The baseline output passes the same reference locations before mutation.

The seven independently exposed SVG defects are now corrected and pass: visible descendants can override inherited hidden visibility; child declarations cannot cancel parent group opacity, clipping or filtering; and malformed numeric transform, polygon and viewBox lists are diagnosed rather than partially parsed. Unsupported CSS geometry is explicitly diagnosed. Further regressions check corrected SVG arc radii, either-zero-radius arcs and physical chord error after scaling. Visibility and group compositing follow the [SVG2 rendering model](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/render.html); numeric-list expectations follow the [viewBox definition](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/coords.html#ViewBoxAttribute) and [shape coordinate grammar](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/shapes.html#PolygonElement). These are actual importer tests; final packaged UI import/scaling journeys remain separately required.

An independent review also reproduced altered verified motion being accepted by the native Apply boundary. That boundary now requires the exact complete operations hash before any document mutation. Six independent mutation regressions pass for changed coordinates, feeds, settings, timing, order and a missing certificate; a mutation of the actual solver output is also rejected. The fresh native proof calls `apply_pair` with altered motion in a real application process and confirms no document change. This is native-boundary coverage, not the complete task-panel journey.

The three small successful cutter families requested `0.2` mm per-side clearance but explicitly proposed larger planning clearances of approximately `0.354`, `0.425` and `0.454` mm, respectively. Their recorded realization bounds are input-dependent. These changes are not proof that the original side-fit value was achieved, and cannot be silently accepted as numerical error. The GUI must present the actual proposal, difference and uncertainty and require its explicit acceptance; changed settings require a new acceptance. Positive bottom/backing-gap probes do not erase this side-fit distinction.

`run_pure.py` stores JUnit/logs/source hashes and rejects an unstable source snapshot even when pytest exits successfully. Actual-solver and mutation audits likewise bind a stable executed-source snapshot. The other runners retain JSON inputs/results, source hashes and receipts in their test-run directories. The native OCCT command uses the native integration launcher's isolated profile and real application process; its optional actual-output directory must point to the newly completed fixed flat fixture run. Its environment must permit the actual FreeCAD binary to launch. A successful macro assertion without a fresh matching completion token and normal process exit is not a completed native run.

The recorded native run is reproduced by the integration runner in [`tools/run_native.py`](../tools/run_native.py), not by using a mock `FreeCAD` module. Export audit input must point to that newly completed run; old fixed paths are examples, not evidence that a fresh build passes.

## Closing the release ledger

The lead owns final package/GUI journeys; the integration agent owns native CAM/portability assertions; the independent reviewer owns output-oracle assessments. A row may become `passed` only when every required variant has a run ID, the installed package/source hash, fixture/settings hashes, action/assertion log, actual application/runtime versions, normal process-completion evidence, and artifact hashes. For cutting journeys retain the source SVG, accepted normalized specification, proposal/acceptance identity, native `.FCStd`, exported `.ngc`, relevant screenshots and independent geometry/export reports. Screen appearance alone cannot establish a correct path; a correct native file alone cannot establish that its GUI route works.

Closing E00–E32 requires the final installed GUI's complete import/placement/tool/depth/fit/selection/recalculation/review/Apply lifecycle; negative and stale-state behaviour; durable native editing/export; failure recovery; installation/update/removal; and resource qualification. E25 may be marked explicitly unimplemented if optional regeneration is not shipped. Partial evidence is stored in `supporting_evidence` separately from a row's final `evidence`, with failing receipts retained. Never promote a row just because its ID appeared in a log or its supporting unit tests passed.

Resource qualification requires external monitoring of the GUI plus worker process tree, the six-family corpus and large stock, bounded previews, observed cancellation/recovery under exhaustion, and offline packaged execution. A measured process-tree budget below 4 GiB on a powerful Mac is useful but does not prove smooth use on every computer with 4 GiB total RAM. Any claim about a complete 4 GiB system needs an explicitly recorded constrained environment. Windows, physical machining and broader performance benchmarks remain separate, accurately labelled follow-up validation.

The finite E00–E32 matrix and numerical oracles can provide reproducible evidence for the tested configurations. They cannot establish exhaustive correctness for all possible artwork, tools, machine resolutions, user actions or hardware. Unresolved geometry must remain uncertified; unexecuted journeys must remain planned or blocked with their actual reason.
