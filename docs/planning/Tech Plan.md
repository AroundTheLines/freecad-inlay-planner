### 2.6. Coordinate, fit, and cutter mathematics

Represent each machining setup in a right-handed frame with its own selected origin. Internally use depth positive into the wood; translate to the normal CAM Z convention at the adapter boundary. A selected face must be planar and aligned for three-axis access after setup transformation. Artwork centering follows slab axes and never relocates the work origin.

The qualified Mac implementation captures existing stock only when the selected outside face extrudes to the complete constant-thickness solid. Nonrectangular outlines and through holes are supported. A planar face on a tapered body or a body with hidden recesses is explicitly rejected: a planar top alone is not a certificate for the three-dimensional remaining stock. Keep this initial implementation limitation visible in the handoff; broader arbitrary-solid capture has not been implemented or qualified. Default rectangular slabs remain the primary workflow.

Keep the actual rigid board/plug assembly transformation explicit, including flipping the plug. Verify its scale, handedness, and orientation with asymmetric artwork. Do not independently mirror coordinates in import, target generation, and export. Different stock positions share the same accepted design scale.

Let `D` be nominal maximum pocket depth, `g` bottom glue gap, `b` backing clearance, `H` plug relief-plane-to-tip height, and `c` per-side normal wall clearance. At the nominated assembly pose:

```text
H = D - g + b
D = H + g - b
s = D - g = H - b
board_depth_of_plug_point = s - plug_local_depth
```

Exactly one of `D` and `H` is the driving input. Side clearance changes mating geometry; it is not an additional term in the reference-plane identity. For narrow V regions without a floor at `D`, compute and display local gaps instead of claiming the nominal bottom gap exists everywhere.

Positive clearance describes a nominated assembly pose, which need not mechanically locate the pieces by itself. Calculate the insertion interval and first rigid contact separately. Check the full insertion motion and the actual remaining stock, including backing/root features. Diagnose rigid interference and impossible parameter combinations; wood compression and actual glue behavior remain outside this model.

For a V wall at half-angle `theta` from vertical, a normal clearance `c` corresponds locally to horizontal offset `c / cos(theta)`. A vertical insertion change consumes that clearance at `sin(theta)` per unit travel. Derive and test this in an independent cross-section fixture; do not apply the straight-wall formula unmodified to curved corners or floor transitions.

Model rotational cutters by the lower cutting profile `C(rho)`, height above the tip at radial distance `rho`, and its inverse section radius `R(a)` at axial height `a`:

| Cutter | Profile contract |
| --- | --- |
| Flat end mill, radius `R` | `C(rho)=0` for `0<=rho<=R`, with finite cutting length. |
| V-bit, flat-tip radius `r0`, half-angle `theta` | Flat for `rho<=r0`; otherwise `C(rho)=(rho-r0)/tan(theta)` up to the actual cutting radius. |
| Bull nose, outer radius `R`, corner radius `r`, central flat `f=R-r` | Flat for `rho<=f`; otherwise `C(rho)=r-sqrt(r*r-(rho-f)*(rho-f))`. For `0<=a<=r`, `R(a)=f+sqrt(2*r*a-a*a)`. Above the corner, use `R`. |

Validate dimensions and finite cutting reach. A bull nose with no flat center is a ball nose and remains outside scope. Unknown required geometry is a visible tool-definition error, not an invented diameter or reach. Account for known noncutting shank interference with stock; do not expand this into fixture simulation. Keep maximum depth per pass separate from physical reach and total depth.

### 2.7. SVG normalization and retained design

Parse local SVG using a hardened XML parser. Disable external entities, remote resources, scripts, and external style/font loading. Preserve the original source for comparison. Do not use browser rendering as the source of machining geometry.

Support filled paths, rectangles, circles, ellipses, polygons, nested transforms/groups, compound paths, and holes. Parse all standard path segment commands through the selected path library. Apply the SVG viewport/viewBox and unit transformation before physical scale and placement. Honor supported fill rules and inherited presentation properties. Outline text must already be geometry. Stroke-only shapes, raster content, live text, unsupported masks/clipping/effects, and invalid topology receive source-linked diagnostics. Supported regions in a mixed document remain usable. [SVG paths](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/paths.html), [SVG coordinates and units](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/coords.html)

Flatten curves adaptively using a bounded chord error after physical scaling. Do not use fixed point counts or pixel resolution. Resolve holes and winding consistently, retaining source IDs through Boolean operations. For ambiguous overlaps or malformed boundaries, show the affected region and require a valid retained result; a general SVG repair editor is unnecessary.

Keep original, proposed, accepted, and excluded geometry distinct. Fine selection can use region picking and a zoomable polygon/rectangle selection mask in the face plane. Intersect the mask with valid filled geometry and recompute holes/components. The retained result must remain a valid paired design. Screen pixel size must not set a permanent selection granularity.

Propose machinable boundary modifications using actual cutter accessibility, with matching changes on both halves. Show source-versus-proposal differences before acceptance. The optimizer cannot silently accept, exclude, merge, or simplify artwork. Numerical topology changes are also visible proposals when they alter the retained design.

### 2.8. Paired target construction

Generate both targets from one accepted artwork revision and explicit assembly transform. Use lazy 2.5D target surfaces with polygon access at requested depths. Maintain exact boundary definitions and conservative approximation bounds; a preview mesh is a display artifact, not the source of truth.

**Flat-end workflow.** Form cavity and complementary plug relief from polygon offsets, respecting holes and per-side clearance. Generate candidate matched corner rounding from rolling-disc opening/closing, then independently check accessibility on both the cavity and complementary relief. Offset operations alone do not prove fit or cutter access. Preserve shared plug backing and disconnected artwork registration.

**V workflow.** For an ideal pointed cutter, let `phi` be signed inward distance from the accepted artwork boundary. Start with these principal-wall targets in the corresponding assembly plane:

```text
board_removal_depth = clamp(phi / tan(theta), 0, D)
plug_removal_depth = clamp(s + (c / cos(theta) - phi) / tan(theta), 0, H)
```

These establish the principal tapered walls and capped depths. Then incorporate finite tip radius, cutting reach, corner interactions, glue space, and backing transitions using the real cutter envelope. Recover narrow/collapsing features through adaptive contour refinement and local finishing paths. Verify residual material at capped floors; a V-only tool set does not magically produce a perfectly flat floor with one pass. A selected compatible flat cutter may clear broad areas.

**Bull-nose workflow.** Include the pocket floor fillet, the complementary plug tip/crown rounding, and the plug/backing root fillet. For a locally straight wall with inward distance `x`, the natural pocket floor transition is `D-r+sqrt(r*r-(r-x)*(r-x))` for `0<=x<=r`, reaching `D` beyond the transition. Construct the mating crown with the intended side/bottom clearances and verify general corners using actual sweeps. Check root interference against the board top when backing clearance is small. Generate feasible relief if available; otherwise report the exact incompatible region.

The first positive bull-only fixture is a wide circular or rounded-rectangular inlay, with depth exceeding the corner radius, positive clearances, and backing clearance at least the corner radius. Also require a deliberately incompatible root-clearance fixture. This establishes useful bull-only capability while ensuring the implementation identifies combinations its geometry cannot produce.

In every style, accepted modifications define a new paired target. Remaining uncuttable included geometry prevents Apply. The user may change settings/tools or explicitly exclude it from both halves. Recalculate and validate the retained pair after any such change.

### 2.9. Cutter-domain path generation and quantization

For target removable XY region `M(d)` at depth `d`, a cutter tip at depth `h` has legal centers conceptually defined by:

```text
K(h) = intersection over axial height a of erosion(M(h-a), disc(R(a)))
```

Compute these center domains lazily with planar erosion/intersection. Bound axial intervals conservatively: use the largest radius and the restrictive material region over an interval, then subdivide where necessary. Checking a few arbitrary cutter-height samples is insufficient. The bound must cover V walls, bull corner transitions, finite flute length, and stock-engaged shank geometry.

Generate deterministic constant-depth contour coverage and clipped raster candidates inside these domains. Respect per-tool stepdown and effective operation overrides. Adapt depth/coverage spacing for the cutter shape and residual bound. Add boundary finishing and local medial-tip coverage where the primary contours collapse. Track predecessor material removal so subsequent clearing and finishing use the correct stock state.

Start with **G0/G1-equivalent motion only**. Cutting, feed-controlled noncutting links, rapid travel, plunge/ramp entry, and retract are explicit motion kinds. Use user-entered feeds and spindle settings. Link through cleared space only when the complete cutter envelope is proven clear; otherwise retract above the stock to the configured clearance plane. Check entry and descent too. Do not use an unmodeled direct plunge as a universal fallback.

Machine resolution is a movement lattice, not a display precision or accuracy guarantee. Quantize X and Y separately to the shared XY increment and Z to its independent increment in the chosen setup frame. Remove zero-motion duplicates; coalesce exactly collinear intermediate motions only when effective settings and the verified sweep remain identical. Recheck topology, bounds, pass depth, cutter sweeps, and assembly after quantization. Do not assume separately rounded halves will still fit.

Use a numerical geometry budget derived from resolution, initially `min(qxy,qz)/32`, split among curve flattening, circle approximation, Boolean precision, and sweep interval bounds. Record accumulated error. This is an internal computation bound, not a user-selected visual tolerance and not permission to command sub-resolution motion. If representational limits prevent certification, expose a resolution/geometry conflict. Do not spend glue clearance as an unreported numerical allowance.

Keep numerical proof error, deterministic deviation from quantized motion, and explicitly accepted geometry modifications separate. Resolution does not automatically grant a machining-error or fit allowance. Enclose final swept removal and remaining stock with conservative inner/outer bounds and compare them with the accepted target. Uncertainty crossing a forbidden-material boundary, an unresolved residual, or a requested fit/bounds condition prevents certification. Refine the calculation; if the representable result differs, return a quantified paired modification or settings conflict. Accepting artwork changes never silently changes requested fit inputs.

GEOS precision reduction can collapse narrow polygons or remove details. Compare before/after topology and route material differences through the modification workflow. Shapely's planar predicates and spatial index do not account for Z; use them for broad-phase search only. [Shapely precision behavior](https://shapely.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/shapely.set_precision.html), [STRtree](https://shapely.readthedocs.io/en/stable/strtree.html)

Check material-intersecting cutter sweeps against the selected rectangular machining bounds, including relief around the plug. A center point inside the bounds is insufficient. Above-stock travel has a separate clearance check; image bounds are not an invented machine travel envelope or clamp model.

### 2.10. Per-operation optimization, counts, and timing

Create semantically meaningful operations: one cutter, face, and machining purpose, with any number of coverage/depth passes. Disconnected SVG regions need not each create an operation. Preserve clearing/finishing dependencies and the user-selected tools needed for retained fidelity. A selected cutter with no useful work may be identified as unused.

For each operation, generate a deterministic legal baseline. Compare a bounded set of coverage angles, contour traversal orders, starting points, and permitted entry/link alternatives. Use spatial ordering and bounded local improvements rather than an unbounded global search. Respect cutting direction when reversing a contour would change the intended machining behavior.

Choose the fastest verified candidate under unchanged cutter, target, feeds, pass constraints, and predecessor stock. Prove estimated time is no worse than the baseline under the same model. This is a concrete optimization guarantee; do not claim a mathematical global optimum.

Calculate motion time from length and effective feed, distinguishing cutting, plunge, feed-controlled travel, and rapid assumptions. Use acceleration/rapid limits if supplied; otherwise label the estimate's assumptions. LinuxCNC rapid speed is controller-governed, so an entered rapid value is an estimate input unless native motion actually implements the requested feed. Manual tool changes, setup, glue-up, backing removal, and final finishing are excluded. [LinuxCNC motion semantics](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/html/gcode/g-code.html)

Evaluate independent operation and tool-change limits for each face. Count transitions between physical cutters within that face, excluding the initial load. A→B→A is two changes; speed changes with A remain the same cutter. Three operations on each face satisfy a four-operation limit on each face. Do not pool unused allowance, assume a machining order between faces, hide necessary work, or split/merge unrelated work to game the count. Conflicts identify the affected face and remain user-adjustable product settings.

### 2.11. Removal, assembly, and preview verification

The production verifier consumes the final quantized operation sequence. For a linear tool movement parameterized by `t`, removal depth at XY point `x` is bounded from:

```text
max over t [ tip_depth(t) - C(distance(x, tool_center_xy(t))) ]
```

Use analytic cases for constant-depth straight moves and bounded subdivision for ramps and changing contact. Check the entire segment, not only endpoints. Track material states across operations and generate residual/gouged/uncertain regions with tool and face attribution.

Evaluate assembly from the simulated remaining solids, including the backing, roots, islands, and floor transitions. Validate nonintersection throughout the insertion interval, not just the final pose. Apply monotone heightfield reasoning where valid and interval refinement at transitions. Report actual side/bottom/backing separation ranges, the nominated pose, first rigid contact, and unresolved uncertainty.

Apply requires certification of every included retained region and both mating halves. Calculated blocked regions and proposed modifications remain available for review, while Apply stays disabled until the retained result is accepted and certified. A display-resolution approximation cannot supply missing evidence.

Generate bounded, tiled static preview meshes and section geometry from those results. Show original and retained artwork, cuttable included areas, blocked included areas, exclusions, and accepted modifications. Link operation selection to its cutter, face, and path. Distinguish cutting and noncutting motion. Keep hide/show separate from inclusion.

Preview detail may be reduced during pan/zoom or calculation, but must be labeled and must not determine machining correctness. The detailed selected region and section view use the validated geometry bounds. No animated cutting playback or photorealistic material renderer is required.

### 2.14. Test harness and independent correctness evidence

Create a controller that launches the pinned FreeCAD executables with isolated configuration, module, data, log, and temporary paths. Confirm startup options using the executable's own help. Capture screenshots for failures and visual review, while using geometric/widget/state assertions for objective pass/fail.

Establish three independent layers:

1. **Analytic fixtures.** Hand-derived circle/rectangle flat pairs, straight V walls at several angles, finite-tip V cases, bull floor/crown sections, and root interference. Expected values must not call production offset, fit, or sweep functions.
2. **Independent motion oracle.** Read final emitted motions and compute small-case sweeps with a separate analytic/interval implementation. Corroborate selected cases with FreeCAD's bundled OCC solids, Boolean intersections, and distance checks. Restrict expensive B-rep tests to small fixtures.
3. **Native/export replay.** Save/reopen native output, invoke FreeCAD's ordinary LinuxCNC postprocessor in the harness, independently parse its emitted motions, and compare effective units, coordinates, feeds, spindle settings, tool transitions, modal path-control settings, and order. Include post rounding in the error bounds.

Qualify LinuxCNC output with an explicit **G61 exact-path mode** and sufficient precision through ordinary CAM postprocessor settings. Verify that mode remains effective across the operations. Unrestricted G64 blending does not inherit the straight-segment geometry certificate. Keep time estimates explicit about exact-path deceleration and unknown acceleration. [LinuxCNC path control](https://linuxcnc.org/docs/html/gcode/g-code.html#sec:G64)

Add precision-failure tests for fine metric and inch resolutions. Where native inch output cannot preserve a fixture's coordinates, qualify the equivalent metric output with adequate native precision; keep mixed-unit input support intact. Record the effective settings, and do not claim arbitrary downstream export settings preserve the verified geometry. [Native LinuxCNC postprocessor](https://github.com/FreeCAD/FreeCAD/blob/1.1.3/src/Mod/CAM/Path/Post/scripts/linuxcnc_post.py)

Precision checks must include native serialization before postprocessing. Probe the actual pinned bundle's `Path.toGCode` and save/reload routes, including their six-decimal defaults, even where `Custom.Gcode` can retain more digits. Compare original planner motion, stored Custom text, restored native `Path.Command` values, and independently parsed post output. Account for every conversion's error and reject an uncertifiable round trip; retaining a high-precision input string is not proof that the restored or exported path preserved it.

Add mutation tests that remove a finishing pass, alter a cutter radius, shift a V depth by one Z increment, reverse the plug orientation, or route a link through stock. The independent checks must detect each defect. A verifier that accepts its own injected fault does not establish correctness.

Use seeded metamorphic/property tests for unit equivalence, holes and winding, origin translations, rigid transform round trips, 90-degree rotations, dimensional scaling, separate XY/Z increments, deterministic ordering, and per-face limit independence. Include several artwork phases relative to the same movement lattice, narrow residual strips, and near-zero glue/side clearances. Random cases must supplement, not replace, analytically known cases.

Include explicit expected topology and diagnostic regions. Scale them from approximately 3 inches to 24 inches and use arbitrary representative 1/16-inch, 1/8-inch, and 1/2-inch cutters plus metric definitions. Include flat-only, V-only, flat-plus-V, bull-only, and mixed clearing cases. Use known fixture feeds and fit values solely for reproducible tests, not product defaults.

### 2.15. Memory target and later performance work

The confirmed target is **4 GB of total system RAM for the whole machine**, including the operating system, graphics, FreeCAD, and all extension processes. Hardware qualification uses a 4 GiB RAM configuration. The initial engineering budget is **2 GiB aggregate peak resident memory for FreeCAD plus all extension workers**, including temporary Apply copies; the remaining memory is reserved for the OS and graphics overhead. This budget is a design target to demonstrate, not a measured result.

Use one worker, packed arrays, lazy depth slices, island/segment spatial partitions, fixed-size tiles with cutter-radius halos, bounded caches, and disk-backed motion chunks. Initial worker working-set target is 512 MiB with a 768 MiB ceiling; a preview cache may use at most 128 MiB. Measure native FreeCAD/document overhead and adjust allocations within the aggregate budget, not by increasing the product target.

Never allocate a global XY grid at machine resolution or a full stock voxel volume. A 609.6 mm square at 0.001 mm spacing has roughly 372 billion cells; one float32 layer alone is around 1.49 TB. Screen preview resolution and numerical machining resolution must therefore have separate representations.

Measure the entire process tree externally, including GEOS, NumPy, OCC, native Path objects, source text duplication, undo data, and Apply peaks. Python allocation tracing alone is insufficient. Stream candidates and release losers promptly. Estimate native output memory before Apply using observed per-command overhead and conservative reserve; preserve the draft and explain resource exhaustion rather than silently coarsening geometry, dropping operations, or exceeding the configured limits.

The qualification corpus includes all six SVG families, a 24-inch case at fine resolution, and a dense field with at least 10,000 flattened boundary vertices and 500 disconnected regions. Record exact fixture sizes, motion counts, and settings. This defines a repeatable workload, not an artificial maximum size for user artwork. Extremely large inputs can exhaust resources; they must fail recoverably with honest diagnostics. Claim 4 GiB support only for the documented tested workload envelope.

During implementation, measure aggregate memory throughout native Mac calculate/preview/Apply/save/reopen tests. A constrained Linux container with swap disabled may supply additional pure-worker evidence; it cannot replace the Mac workflow checks. These are regression evidence; a process limit on a powerful machine does not by itself prove a whole 4 GiB desktop is usable.
